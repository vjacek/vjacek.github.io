A Crown Imperiled

August 5, 2026

Raymond E. Feist

★ ★ ★ ★ ★

Fantasy books that contain maps are good. Fantasy books that require maps are great! This one traipses all over Midkemia for good story-driven and story-advancing reasons. We learn about the Island of Snake (men), the Girdle of Kesh, Crydee and the Free Cities, then travel east to Krondor, Rillanon, and Roldem. After having read something like 15 books set in Midkemia, I have a mental map of Midkemia to rival what I could draw of the United States from memory, or even the lands of the Wheel of Time, a place where I adventured for 15 years. Getting to know a world at both the high level of maps and at the stories that occur within a single dot is wonderful.

Feist writes incredible and intense bargaining scenes, in this book’s case Jim Dasher’s need for information meets Bill the Butcher’s desire to place his son at the head of the Mockers. This is a callback to one of my favorite literary scenes of all time: when Prince Arutha bargains the Upright Man’s Mocker’s help for the cost of a squireship for Jimmy the Hand.

Hilariously, Bill’s son is also named James and will take over the Mockers… Feist is just being intentionally funny at this point! Every thief of note is named Jim, James, or Jimmy. He tried to introduce the character “Jommy” as a new generation of Jimmy the Hand but it didn’t land very well, and then went back to Jim Dasher. I’m sure it’s no small coincidence that Feist’s own son is named… you guessed it, James.

We are once again reminded of Pug’s bargain with Lims Kragma: Pug will live to see everyone he loves die. Knowing this is the second-to-last book in Midkemia that Feist will ever write, I’m very interested in seeing how this plays out. I have a theory that you could use this bargain to become immortal, by learning to expand the circle of people you love. But the price would be having to continuously lose them. I’m not sure this will be the path the books take, but it would certainly be an interesting and difficult decision.

In fiction, I find it awesome when one author references another’s work. In A Crown Imperiled, Feist has his characters reference the Wheel of Time in universe! To me, this is the ultimate sign of respect from an author.

Lastly, there’s a few scenes that are just regular very cool instead of being incredible. Dragons waking up from being hidden in plain sight of human form; this is such a great new way to answer the question: “Where did all the dragons go?”. In addition, we’ve gotten many descriptions of the lower energy states that comprise the hells of Midkemia, but now finally see the elevated energy states of heaven mirrored as Rider is sent as a messenger. And of course, we can’t forget the good old-fashioned Princess-saving that takes place too; this book really has a bit of everything.
